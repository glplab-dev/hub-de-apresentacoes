02 · Context

NORM in the
Oil & Gas industry

During oil and gas exploration, production and processing, naturally occurring radioactive materials can become concentrated in fluids, scale, sludge, sediments and operational waste.

These materials, known as NORM (Naturally Occurring Radioactive Material), may be present at different stages of the operation and require investigation, characterization and proper management.

04 · Application

Offshore operations
and FPSOs

On FPSOs, oil tankers and other offshore units, waste can accumulate in tanks, piping and equipment during operation.

During cleaning and maintenance activities, sludge, scale and other materials are removed and packaged for subsequent characterization.

Radiometric analysis makes it possible to identify the presence and concentration of natural radionuclides, providing technical information for the classification and management of these materials.

Produced water

Produced water is one of the main matrices of radiological interest in the oil and gas industry. GLP performs analyses for the identification and quantification of natural radionuclides, making it possible to assess the presence of radioactivity associated with the geological characteristics of the reservoir and production processes.

10 · Final cycle

Decommissioning

The decommissioning of oil and gas facilities requires detailed knowledge of the conditions of the materials and structures involved. Radiological characterization can support the identification of materials containing NORM before processes of:

1 · Removal 2 · Transport 3 · Storage 4 · Reuse 5 · Treatment 6 · Final disposal

The analytical results provide a technical basis for planning the next stages of the process.

Analytical techniques

The choice of methodology depends on the matrix, the radionuclides of interest and the objectives of the investigation.

Gamma Spectrometry

Used in specific analyses depending on the matrix and radionuclide.

Proportional Counter

Gas-flow counter for specific applications.
